\begin{abstract}
Probabilistic crowd navigation repeatedly evaluates collision risk for candidate
trajectories that share the same predictive posterior. Discarding uncertainty can reduce
computation but also change decisions. We instead study how to preserve a specified
finite-budget model predictive controller while avoiding irrelevant probability evaluations.
Shared cubic-Hermite envelopes exploit candidate-independent Gaussian-disc parameters.
Their bounds propagate through the existing risk aggregation and feasibility ordering;
only candidates that can affect route elites or the controller's tolerance-based final
choice require exact refinement. We derive a real-arithmetic interpolation bound and
conditional search-preservation argument, separately from floating-point certification.
On 30 development replays with 1358 control states, protected Hermite evaluation preserves
ordered elites, sampling distributions, iteration winners and complete controls.
Three balanced timing passes reduce median planning latency in the 20-person square
configuration from 140.84 to 125.15 ms; cached rectangle bounds require 140.34 ms.
Direct interpolation is faster still at 104.11 ms and differs in output on only one
state across the cohort, by $1.11\times10^{-16}$. Thus natural replays do not establish
a practically significant decision advantage over direct interpolation. Historical
navigation ablations separately motivate retaining uncertainty, while an independent
60-layout budget pilot finds lower computation time but no navigation improvement.
The contribution is a specialized planning-computation mechanism, not a new Bayesian
filter, global optimality theorem, or real-world safety guarantee.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{Bayesian navigation; Model predictive control; Collision probability; Hermite interpolation; Cross-entropy method; Decision preservation}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
Robots navigating among pedestrians evaluate many candidate motions under uncertain
predictions. A posterior can be useful when pedestrians are intermittently observed:
a remembered position does not express uncertainty accumulated during an observation
gap. However, keeping this information creates repeated numerical work. Many robot
candidates query the same pedestrian prediction at the same future step, differing
only in distance from its mean.

This paper addresses a computational question:
\emph{can a navigator retain its posterior, risk criteria, sampling budget and selected
motions while avoiding probability calculations that cannot change its search?}
We do not change the crowd model, add a policy network, or tune the risk profile for
a higher success rate. Instead, we exploit repeated structure in Gaussian-disc risk.

For a fixed predictive posterior, the ratio of collision radius to standard deviation
is independent of the robot candidate. We tabulate the resulting one-dimensional
probability function and propagate interpolation intervals to the controller's ranking
criteria. Exact computation is reserved for candidates that could enter a route's elite
set or affect its iteration winner. Preserving the final action of one batch is not
enough: an intermediate elite change alters the next sampling distribution.
Our method consequently preserves ordered elite updates at every iteration and
separately handles the controller's clearance-tolerance decisions.

The principal contribution is this shared probability-envelope and refinement mechanism.
Bayesian tracking, route initialization, actuator constraints, costs and fallback motion
define the inherited controller; they are not separate innovation claims.
The theoretical statement is conditional on valid bounds, common random numbers and
identical ordering conventions. Numerical validation is reported separately because
small outward padding is not a floating-point proof.

We distinguish posterior uncertainty from numerical uncertainty: the former describes
where a pedestrian may be, whereas the latter describes the accuracy with which a fixed
probability has been evaluated. Our method preserves the former and spends computation
on the latter only where it can affect a selection.

Three evidence cohorts answer separate questions. Historical six-scene experiments
motivate retaining posterior information. Two-layer replay compares exact evaluation,
cached rectangles, direct interpolation and protected Hermite evaluation on fixed inputs.
A population-only budget experiment separately checks whether acceleration purchases
navigation improvement. Its two arms select the same population and have identical
navigation outcomes. These cohorts are not combined into a new 97.17\% success claim
for the accelerated implementation.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\subsection{Bounded Collision Probability}
Thomas, Mastrogiovanni and Baglietto derive exact and bounded collision probabilities
under Gaussian uncertainty for ellipsoidal geometry
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06348}{Exact and Bounded Collision Probability for Motion Planning under Gaussian Uncertainty}).
Their Section IV-B also explicitly discusses noncentral-chi-square table lookup;
neither lookup nor fast probability bounds are new here. Their IV-C upper bound
requires a finite support bound, which cannot silently be imposed on our untruncated
Gaussian event. The distinction studied here is a
shared fixed-ratio disk query coupled to candidate refinement within finite CEM
iterations, not the first use of collision-probability bounds.

\subsection{Decision-Preserving Simplification}
Elimelech and Indelman develop decision simplification and action consistency, including
belief sparsification dedicated to planning
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00885}{Simplified decision making in the belief space using belief sparsification}).
Zhitnikov and Indelman study adaptive candidate acceptance and rejection under
probabilistic belief-dependent constraints
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06697}{Simplified Continuous High Dimensional Belief Space Planning with Adaptive Probabilistic Belief-dependent Constraints}).
In particular, their equations (22)--(31) propagate bounds through probabilistic
constraints and tighten unresolved acceptance or rejection decisions. We supply a
particular shared disk-probability construction and its scoring-interface propagation,
not a new general principle of bound-based screening.
We preserve the predictive posterior and partially resolve risk ordering in a finite
search, rather than simplifying the initial belief or expanding future observation trees.
General action-order preservation can already imply elite preservation; induction across
CEM iterations is not, by itself, a fundamentally new decision-consistency theory.

\subsection{Lazy Cross-Entropy Search}
LCEOPT uses policy-tree structure to avoid redundant work in sampling, evaluation and
cross-entropy updates
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08049}{A Surprisingly Simple Continuous-Action POMDP Solver: Lazy Cross-Entropy Search Over Policy Trees}).
Our avoided work is risk refinement for trajectory candidates, rather than evaluation
of unvisited policy-tree components. Bounded probability, decision preservation and
lazy CEM are therefore not independent novelty claims. The experiment below tests our
concrete envelope against a cached geometric bound using the same selector, not against
all these complete planning systems.

\section{BASE NAVIGATOR}
\subsection{Observation and Recursive Belief}
Inputs comprise identity-associated position and velocity detections, radii and visibility
evidence. Hidden true positions and pedestrian goals are not planner inputs.
Inter-agent occlusion and limited sensing range can both remove pedestrians from
observation; whether pedestrians react to the robot is a separate simulator setting.

Track $i$ is represented by $(r_t^i,\mu_t^i,P_t^i)$ with existence probability,
conditional Gaussian state and missed-observation metadata. Its inherited prediction is
\begin{align}
F&=\begin{bmatrix}I_2&\Delta t I_2\\0&I_2\end{bmatrix},\\
Q&=\sigma_a^2\begin{bmatrix}
\Delta t^4 I_2/4&\Delta t^3 I_2/2\\
\Delta t^3 I_2/2&\Delta t^2 I_2
\end{bmatrix},\\
\mu_t^-&=F\mu_{t-1}^+,\quad
P_t^-=FP_{t-1}^+F^\top+Q,\quad r_t^-=p_Sr_{t-1}^+ .
\end{align}
Noisy detections use the Kalman update in Joseph covariance form. For exact observations,
the state is set to the measurement and covariance to zero. Detections reset missed age
and existence confidence to 0.999.

If the predicted mean should be visible but no detection occurs,
\begin{equation}
r_t^+=\frac{(1-p_D)r_t^-}{1-p_Dr_t^-}.
\end{equation}
This mean-location query is not an integral of detection probability over the posterior.
Inherited low-existence and missed-age thresholds determine deletion. No spatial birth
model for never-observed pedestrians is enabled.

Future means and covariances use the same $F,Q$ recursion, while current existence
weights are retained over the horizon. Following an exact observation,
$P_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}F^jQ(F^j)^\top$; uncertainty growth is then determined by prediction
age, not learned intent. These assumptions limit the interpretation of the historical
Bayesian ablation.

\subsection{Risk and Geometry}
For robot candidate $j$, pedestrian $i$ and step $k$, let $d_{j,i,k}$ denote distance
to the predicted mean, and let $R_i=r_r+r_i+m_h$ include the inherited margin.
For isotropic covariance $\sigma_{i,k}^2I_2$,
\begin{equation}
q_{j,i,k}=
F_{\chi'^2_2(d_{j,i,k}^2/\sigma_{i,k}^2)}
 (R_i^2/\sigma_{i,k}^2).
\label{eq:disc}
\end{equation}
The new core rejects arbitrary anisotropic covariances rather than silently treating
an isotropic approximation as exact integration. It retains the reference's variance
floor, deterministic convention and positive 8-sigma tail substitution. Numerical
preservation is relative to these conventions, not an unmodified analytic tail.

Existence-weighted components are accumulated as
\begin{equation}
h_{j,k}=-\sum_i\log\left(1-
 \operatorname{clip}(r_t^iq_{j,i,k},0,1-10^{-12})\right).
\label{eq:hazard}
\end{equation}
This factorization ignores dependent pedestrian motion. Marginal step constraints
also do not constitute a joint trajectory-level collision guarantee.
The inherited budget remains
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_k=\epsilon_{\rm near}
+(\epsilon_{\rm far}-\epsilon_{\rm near})
\left(\frac{k-1}{H-1}\right)^2,\qquad
\lambda_k=-\log(1-\epsilon_k).
\end{equation}
Swept mean geometry, margins, goal masking and penalties remain part of the base
navigator. The acceleration study neither removes these rules nor claims they are
individually optimal.

\subsection{Fixed Search and Three Selection Rules}
The audited holonomic controller uses $H=16$, $\Delta t=0.25$\,s, population 512,
four iterations and elite fraction 0.08. Maximum speed and acceleration are 1.0\,m/s
and 2.0\,m/s$^2$. Its calculation configuration uses $m_h=0.10$\,m,
$\epsilon_{\rm near}=0.225$, $\epsilon_{\rm far}=0.75$ and risk weight 1.0.
These are recorded effective values, not class defaults.

Half the population follows the warm-start distribution; the remaining budget follows
the inherited left, right and wait-then-go routes. Noise correlation, candidate
projection, seed insertion and route-wise distribution updates are unchanged.
Risk is appended to a common non-risk cost in the original arithmetic order:
\begin{equation}
J_j=J_{{\rm base},j}
+w_h\sum_k A_{j,k}h_{j,k}
+10^5\ind\{\max_{k:A_{j,k}=1}(h_{j,k}-\lambda_k)>0\},
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $A$ masks steps after goal entry. The base cost retains goal, smoothness,
effort, stagnation and geometry contributions.

Let $f_j,s_j$ denote full-horizon and first-step clearance including risk slack.
Elite selection uses lexicographic keys
\begin{equation}
K_j=(c_j,v_j,J_j),\quad
c_j=\begin{cases}0&f_j\ge0\\1&f_j<0,\ s_j\ge0\\2&s_j<0,\end{cases}
\quad
v_j=\begin{cases}0&c_j=0\\-f_j&c_j=1\\-s_j&c_j=2.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The iteration winner is instead lowest-cost full-feasible; otherwise lowest-cost
first-feasible within 0.02 of maximum horizon clearance; otherwise lowest-cost within
0.02 of maximum first-step physical clearance. Cross-iteration replacement retains
its original tolerance ordering. These three responsibilities are distinct; the
first elite cannot generally substitute for the execution candidate.

\section{SHARED ENVELOPES AND ELITE PRESERVATION}
\subsection{Shared Query Structure}
Define
\begin{equation}
a_{i,k}=R_i/\sigma_{i,k},\qquad
z_{j,i,k}=(d_{j,i,k}-R_i)/\sigma_{i,k}.
\end{equation}
Each $a_{i,k}$ is fixed across candidates and search iterations in one planning call,
whereas $z_{j,i,k}$ changes. Equation~\eqref{eq:disc} becomes
\begin{equation}
f_a(z)=F_{\chi'^2_2((a+z)^2)}(a^2).
\end{equation}
A table for every distinct $a$ is shared over that call, using standardized spacing
$h=0.125$. It is rebuilt on the next observation. This is shared numerical work,
not posterior compression or a new cross-time prediction cache.

\subsection{Error Envelope}
Differentiating Gaussian measure over a translated disk gives a fourth derivative
of the form $\E[\ind_A He_4(Z_x)]$. Since $\E He_4=0$ and $\E He_4^2=24$,
\begin{equation}
|f_a^{(4)}|
=\left|\E[(\ind_A-\tfrac12)He_4]\right|
\le\tfrac12\E|He_4|\le\sqrt{24}/2.
\end{equation}
The cubic-Hermite remainder therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}
|f_a-\widetilde f_a|
\le\delta_{\rm interp}=\sqrt{24}h^4/768,
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
approximately $1.56\times10^{-6}$ at the selected spacing.

This is a real-arithmetic result. With certified node-value error $e_0$, derivative
error $e_1$ and polynomial error $e_r$, one sufficient query budget is
\begin{equation}
\delta=\sqrt{24}h^4/768+e_0+(h/4)e_1+e_r.
\end{equation}
The value basis has absolute sum one; the derivative basis has absolute sum at most
one quarter. Monotone endpoint clipping and hazard aggregation also require rounding
accounting. A universal SciPy CDF/Bessel error bound has not been established here.
Small outward padding and empirical consistency do not provide implementation-level
formal certification.

\subsection{Refining Only Relevant Candidates}
The aggregation in \eqref{eq:hazard} is nondecreasing for existence in $[0,1]$.
With nonnegative risk weight, probability intervals yield optimistic and pessimistic
costs and clearances through one shared scoring implementation.
Let $L_j,U_j$ be the associated key bounds.
For a route requiring $k$ elites, let $\tau$ be its $k$th smallest pessimistic key.
Exclude candidate $j$ from elite consideration only when
\begin{equation}
L_j\succ_{\rm lex}\tau.
\label{eq:exclude}
\end{equation}
At least $k$ true keys are no worse than $\tau$, while this candidate is strictly worse.
All ties survive. Relevant candidates are refined using the original exact evaluator,
and the original sorting rule recovers the ordered elites.

The method additionally refines potential iteration winners in the full-feasible
and clearance-tolerance pools. It never substitutes an interpolation midpoint for an
exact selected score. An explicit mask checks that selected elites and winners have
been resolved. Nonfinite or reversed bounds on valid inputs trigger exact evaluation;
invalid inputs are errors, not zero-risk candidates.

\subsection{Conditional Preservation}
Assume valid bounds, identical inputs and warm starts, common random numbers and
unchanged arithmetic, numbering and tie-breaking. The initial candidates coincide.
Strict exclusion cannot remove a true elite, and exact refinement resolves the
remaining relevant comparisons. Ordered elite indices are unchanged, so the original
mean and deviation reductions yield the same next sampling distributions and candidates.
Induction preserves every finite iteration. Independently preserved iteration winners
and cross-iteration replacement yield the same complete control sequence.

The claim does not require the full ordering of discarded candidates and does not
establish global planning optimality. Equality on fixed inputs also does not imply
identical physical trajectories in asynchronous deployments where latency changes
future observation or command timing.

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
\caption{Shared-envelope evaluation within one CEM loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Validate the observation and construct per-call envelopes
\For{each inherited CEM iteration}
\State Generate and project the original route-wise candidates
\State Compute common geometry and non-risk cost
\State Obtain optimistic and pessimistic risk scores
\State Retain possible elites by strict exclusion only
\State Retain possible winners of the original tolerance pools
\State Refine retained candidates with original exact risk
\State Apply the unchanged three selection rules
\State Update route distributions in the original order
\EndFor
\State \Return first control of the best evaluated sequence
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Computational Balance}
The relevant cost balance is
\begin{align}
T_E&=T_{\rm common}+T_{\rm all\ exact},\\
T_H&=T_{\rm common}+T_{\rm table}+T_{\rm bounds}
     +T_{\rm screen}+T_{\rm remaining\ exact}.
\end{align}
Geometry still visits all candidates. An approximately 8\% refined-candidate fraction
is not an 8\% total-operation fraction: exact evaluation already masks distant
components, and table creation invokes both CDF and derivative functions.
No reduction of the population-by-human asymptotic order is claimed.

The runtime contains one Bayesian track recursion and one MPC loop; Hermite is an
internal risk operation. Exact, rectangle and Hermite arms share the loop and scoring
formula. Unused density recursion, dormant occupancy output and unconsumed Bayes
uncertainty-buffer calculations are removed, but visibility evidence and track
management remain. Shared cleanup savings are not attributed exclusively to Hermite.

Direct interpolation is a distinct experimental control, denoted T. It shares the
same nodes and derivatives but scores the cubic point value without intervals or
selective refinement. The protection overhead is $T_H-T_T$; H is not required to be
faster than this unprotected approximation. Small pointwise error alone cannot establish
selection equivalence near discontinuous feasibility penalties or nearly tied elites.

\section{EXPERIMENTS}
\subsection{Protocol and Reference Independence}
The computational study uses 30 previously collected development episodes: ten 5-person
circles, ten 10-person circles, and ten 20-person squares, totaling 1358 control states.
Crowd count and layout geometry both change; these are configurations, not an isolated
density manipulation. Inputs, seeds, effective settings and source hashes are registered.
This cohort is not a fresh held-out confirmation set.

An earlier architecture regression compares the common implementation against retained
untouched exact and Hermite references. The present experiment freezes that implementation
and changes only its experimental evaluator. E uses original exact risk; R uses cached
rectangle bounds and selective refinement; T directly scores the same cubic table used
by H; H uses its envelope and necessary refinement. T does not construct and discard
intervals or evaluate every candidate exactly before returning an approximation.

The first replay layer evaluates E's actual candidate batches with all four methods.
Posterior, projected trajectories and geometric terms are fixed, isolating numerical
evaluation from search evolution. Reference scores audit exclusions after screening;
they are not supplied to R or H when deciding what to refine. We record probability
errors, category changes, ordered elites and the iteration winner. Refinement counts
partition into elite candidates, selection-only candidates outside that set, unresolved
but ultimately unselected candidates, and excluded candidates.

The second layer supplies common recorded posteriors but permits each method to generate
its own candidates, update distributions and retain its own warm start. E's distribution
is never imposed on T after a disagreement. We compare ordered elites, means and
deviations, iteration winners, cross-iteration best and complete controls, including
the first differing iteration and maximum numerical control difference. These are
search replays, not four new closed-loop navigation rollouts.

\subsection{Timing and Statistical Units}
Three pure timing passes run separately from detailed tracing, with serialization outside
measured calls. Numerical-library threads are fixed to one, methods run serially on an
AMD Ryzen 7 5700G, and invocation order is balanced across all permutations. The
environment uses Python 3.8, NumPy 1.23.5 and SciPy 1.10.1; CPU frequency is not locked.
A full planning call
includes table construction and remaining exact refinement, but not the upstream filter.
Step percentiles pool measured calls across the three passes, rather than averaging
episode percentiles. Paired mean differences first average technical repetitions within
each episode; exploratory 95\% intervals bootstrap the ten paired episodes per
configuration. Technical repetitions and correlated control states are not independent
navigation samples. Slow steps and slower episodes remain in the report.

Detailed mechanism measurements count table CDF and derivative evaluations as well as
candidate CDF evaluations. Exclusive subroutine timers in that instrumented pass explain
work allocation, but are not substituted for the uninstrumented latency measurements.
The protection cost is the paired H-minus-T time, not an assumed advantage of H over T.

\subsection{Upstream and Numerical Checks}
Posterior-only replays cannot validate tracker simplification. Six deterministic
legal-observation fixtures separately cover continuous detections, occlusion and
reappearance, visible misses, deletion, measurement noise and timestamp gaps.
These short sequences compare ID order, full means and covariances, existence,
missed age, visibility, predictions and adapter outputs.
They are not additional navigation episodes.
Separate tests reject invalid inputs and confirm that a failed envelope returns
the same controls as exact evaluation on valid input.

\section{RESULTS}
\subsection{Information Value}
The historical controlled comparison in Table~\ref{tab:historical} answers why posterior
uncertainty is retained: with the same track set and means, collision counts fall from
39 to 12 when uncertainty is used. This motivates the computational problem without
claiming a new estimator or Bayesian superiority over every uncertainty representation.
The full historical and severe-noise results are retained below; their version and
layouts are not conflated with this replay cohort.

\subsection{Fixed-Candidate and Evolving-Search Preservation}
The first layer audits 5432 actual E candidate batches, comprising 2781184 candidate
rows. R and H preserve all ordered route elites and iteration winners, with no
incorrectly excluded necessary candidates. No reference hazard lies outside the
recorded intervals, even before the registered $10^{-10}$ diagnostic tolerance.
This empirical enclosure result is distinct from a universal floating-point bound.

T changes no feasibility category, but changes eight ordered route-elite lists and
seven iteration-winner indices in the 10-person configuration. Its maximum conditional
probability error is $4.364\times10^{-7}$, below the real-arithmetic interpolation bound;
the maximum aggregated hazard error is $3.354\times10^{-5}$. Small error therefore does
not imply identical discrete selections.

In the second layer, R and H preserve the complete search traces and controls on all
1358 states. T changes eight iteration elite collections and seven iteration winners,
yet changes the final control sequence and first action on only one state, at a maximum
absolute component difference of $1.1102230246251565\times10^{-16}$. The first trace
disagreement is case 23000, step 33, iteration zero in the 10-person configuration.
No practically significant control discrepancy or navigation loss is demonstrated.
The grid was not coarsened to manufacture one.

Earlier old/new architecture checks against untouched references and 96 upstream
observation updates remain separate provenance evidence. They are not added to the
current sample count. The present replay establishes tested preservation of the frozen
implementation, not new tracker validity, global optimality or physical safety.

\subsection{Why Tight Bounds Reduce Refinement}
In the 20-person configuration, R refines 476925 of 1060864 fixed candidate rows
(44.96\%); H refines 85140 (8.03\%). Both include 84952 elite rows. R additionally
refines 7000 nonelite selection-pool rows and 384973 ultimately unused rows; H has
58 and 130, respectively. R leaves 148692 candidate categories unresolved by its
bounds, versus nine for H. This is evidence that tight bounds reduce unresolved
comparisons, not that H ignores more posterior mass.

The nonelite selection-pool counts need not match: a broad exact tolerance pool is
an audit category, not a claim that every member must be refined. H can exclude
nonwinning members while preserving the winner. Similarly, all-resolved masks on
12 no-risk batches in the 5-person cohort do not represent CDF refinement. Their
6144 rows are excluded from the bound-evaluated partition in Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism},
but retained in timing and selection audits. Raw masks remain in the records.

The first naturally encountered 20-person feasible elite boundary is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:boundary}. The methods receive identical candidates. This local view
and the width-stratified counts are descriptive, not a causal claim that interval
width alone controls runtime or that larger covariance always makes screening useful.

\subsection{Special-Function Accounting}
Across the 518 independently evolving 20-person search calls, E performs 192378140
candidate CDF scalar evaluations; R performs 85975523; H performs 15379103. H also
uses 1860309 table CDF and 1860309 scaled-Bessel evaluations. T uses the same table
counts and 1035 outside-table reference CDF evaluations. The mean number of distinct
curve parameters is 27.84 per call, each with 129 nodes. Curve equality is exact;
parameters are not rounded into bins.
There are 2072 bound-query batches, or 276725760 candidate--pedestrian--step components
before the shared numerical conventions are applied. The corresponding counts in the

Diagnostic H subroutine means in milliseconds are 55.50 for the instrumented common

\section{DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS}
The implementation removes duplicate search logic and dormant computations, not the
base navigator's active decision rules. Route populations, geometry and degraded motion
still affect behavior. The claim is relative to this declared controller, not a
hypothetical minimal MPC.

A tighter envelope is useful only when avoided refinement outweighs lookup and screening.
Common geometry remains substantial. The direct-interpolation control is a stronger
challenge than rectangles: it is faster and virtually output-identical on these inputs.
The present evidence supports a costed conditional selection guarantee, not an observed
material control advantage over an ordinary lookup table. No adaptive switching
mechanism or deliberately coarsened table is added to conceal that tradeoff.

The probability model is isotropic and factorized across pedestrians. Actual motion
need not satisfy independence, and the sign of positional correlation alone does not
determine union-risk bias. The model does not infer intentions, ambiguous identities
or never-seen pedestrian births. Preserving decisions does not repair these limitations.

A universal floating-point enclosure for the employed special functions remains
unestablished. Development replays do not validate physical deployment, unicycle
execution or asynchronous timing. The separate independent budget pilot does not
extend this four-evaluator mechanism study to held-out posteriors or establish extra
navigation benefit. Timing repetitions share one machine with normal desktop services
and unlocked CPU frequency. No empirical superiority over complete contemporary
belief-space planners is claimed.

Existing work already establishes bounded collision probabilities, action-consistent
simplification and lazy cross-entropy search. The proposed specialization is shared
Gaussian-risk evaluation with explicit elite and tolerance-pool refinement, evaluated
through a controlled cost comparison. Its publication-level novelty cannot be inferred
from a name, a software cleanup or a positive speedup alone.

\section{CONCLUSION}
Shared Hermite envelopes and selective exact refinement reduce repeated probability work
without discarding the navigator's posterior or changing its tested finite-search decisions.
The maintained implementation has one search loop and one risk-scoring formula.
Two-layer E/R/T/H replay separates local evaluation differences from evolving-search
effects. H preserves the tested reference and reduces computation, whereas direct
interpolation is faster with only a floating-point-scale output discrepancy. The
measured protection overhead must therefore be justified by the conditional selection
property, not by an invented navigation improvement. Historical ablations motivate
retaining uncertainty; the independent budget pilot explicitly finds no additional
navigation benefit. The demonstrated objective is efficient preservation of a specified
controller, not new Bayesian inference or guaranteed safety.
